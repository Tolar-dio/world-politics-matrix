Part 2 covers 22 of the world's most powerful states, rewritten in the storytelling style used from Part 5 on: the history that gives the year its stakes, then 2026's events, then how power works and who holds it. Current to 12 October 2026. The original Part 2's "Iran supplement" is dropped; Iran is covered in full in the rewritten Part 1.

Two threads run through almost every entry. The first is the Iran war: it emptied the Suez Canal, put Houthi missiles on Saudi cities, raised Indonesia's fuel prices and made Pakistan Washington's indispensable go-between. The second is the incumbent squeeze: Starmer gone, Merz shaken by the AfD, Meloni beaten at a referendum, Albanese's opposition overtaken by One Nation, Mamata Banerjee ousted. Where incumbents held firm, as in Russia, Turkey and Egypt, it was because opponents were barred, jailed or never allowed to run.

## Africa

### Egypt

In 1956, Gamal Abdel Nasser nationalised the Suez Canal, and Britain, France and Israel invaded to take it back. Egypt kept it, and the canal became a symbol of independence and, more practically, one of its few reliable sources of hard currency. In its record year, 2022-23, it earned $10.2 billion. Then the Houthis started firing at ships in the Red Sea after the Gaza war began, shipping lines diverted around Africa, and revenue fell by about two-thirds. President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi says the disruption has cost Egypt roughly $10 billion ([Alhurra](https://alhurra.com/en/15269)).

Early 2026 looked like recovery: the canal earned $449 million in the first weeks of the year, up 18.5 percent, and tourism hit a record $24 billion in 2025 ([Middle East Institute](https://mei.edu/publication/battered-but-still-standing-egypt-tries-to-weather-the-economic-ravages-of-the-iran-war/)). In February Sisi reshuffled the cabinet, bringing in 13 new ministers including a new defence minister and a World Bank economist as planning minister ([AP via The Messenger](https://www.the-messenger.com/news/world/article_db7f4b39-3154-552c-9e06-baed068253b5.html)). Then the US-Israeli war on Iran began on 28 February. Maersk again halted voyages through the Red Sea, freight and insurance costs jumped, tourists cancelled, Israeli gas supplies were interrupted and about $10 billion of foreign portfolio money fled the country ([NoonPost](https://www.noonpost.com/en/373816/)). Egypt was better prepared than in past shocks thanks to IMF-backed reforms and big Gulf investment deals, but for 110 million people with little margin, the effect shows up in bread and fuel prices. Sisi has worked to keep Egypt out of the war and positioned it as a mediator.

**How power works:** A presidential republic dominated by the military. The president, a former army chief, is elected for six-year terms in tightly controlled votes and appoints the prime minister; parliament is overwhelmingly loyal. Dissent is heavily repressed.

**Who holds power:** President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi, in power since 2013; Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly.

### Nigeria

Nigeria spent most of its first 40 years of independence under military rule, with six successful coups between 1966 and 1993. Since 1999 it has been a democracy, with power changing hands at the ballot box. In 2026, Nigerians learned how close that came to ending.

On 29 September 2025, the government abruptly cancelled the military parade for the 65th Independence Day. Officials denied any coup plot. Only in January 2026 did the military admit it: officers had planned to seize power and, investigators said, to kill President Bola Tinubu, Vice President Kashim Shettima, the Senate president and the House speaker ([Premium Times via AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202601300056.html)). Some 40 suspects were identified, including officers, a police inspector and civilians accused of funding and propaganda; a Nollywood filmmaker was arrested as an alleged "propagandist," and a former oil minister, Timipre Sylva, accused of bankrolling the plot, remains at large ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/africa/2026-04-24-behind-nigerias-murky-coup-plot-money-prayers-and-a-nollywood-arrest)). Six civilians were indicted in April and 36 military personnel went before a court martial in May. Analysts say the bigger story is why the plot was hidden, and what it says about an army fighting jihadists in the northeast, bandits in the northwest and separatists in the southeast with mixed success; one told RFI security "has never been worse" ([RFI via AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202605110056.html)). US drones and troops now help with intelligence. Tinubu, whose reforms ended fuel subsidies and sent prices soaring, is running for a second term in 2027.

**How power works:** A federal presidential republic of 36 states. The president is directly elected for four years with a two-term limit and must win a quarter of the vote in two-thirds of the states; the National Assembly has a Senate and a House. State governors are powerful.

**Who holds power:** President Bola Tinubu (APC), in office since 2023.

### South Africa

For 30 years after Nelson Mandela's election in 1994, the African National Congress governed South Africa on its own. In 2024, worn down by corruption, power cuts and unemployment above 30 percent, it lost its majority for the first time, and Cyril Ramaphosa formed a Government of National Unity with its old rival, the business-friendly Democratic Alliance, the party many black South Africans long saw as representing white interests ([Friedrich Naumann Foundation](https://www.freiheit.org/liberal-workshop-south-africa/south-african-2026-local-government-election)). Against expectations, the coalition has mostly held. In 2026 it faces its first big test at the ballot box.

Municipal elections are on 4 November, and they matter more than usual because South Africa's cities are where government visibly fails: water cuts, collapsing roads and uncollected rubbish. The DA chose a new leader, Geordin Hill-Lewis, the mayor of Cape Town, in April, and is running former party leader Helen Zille for mayor of Johannesburg, the economic hub ([Financial Mail](https://fm.co.za/tags/elections/)). One poll in Tshwane, home to the capital Pretoria, had the DA on 43 percent and the ANC on 32. The ANC is squeezed from all sides: its ally the Communist Party is running separately in protest at the coalition, and Jacob Zuma's MK party and the EFF compete for its base ([EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/01/26/anc-succession-talks-heat-up-da-leadership-contest-looms)). Analysts expect a record number of hung councils. Meanwhile, the ANC is quietly fighting over who succeeds Ramaphosa, and tensions with the Trump administration weigh on trade.

**How power works:** A parliamentary republic. The 400-member National Assembly, elected by proportional representation, elects the president, who is both head of state and government, for up to two five-year terms. The nine provinces and the municipalities have their own elected governments.

**Who holds power:** President Cyril Ramaphosa (ANC), leading the Government of National Unity since 2024.

## Americas

### Argentina

A century ago Argentina was one of the ten richest countries on Earth. Since then it has defaulted on its debt nine times and lived through hyperinflation, and by 2023 prices were rising more than 200 percent a year. Voters responded by electing Javier Milei, a wild-haired libertarian economist who campaigned with a chainsaw and called himself an "anarcho-capitalist." Few expected him to last. In 2026 he looked stronger than ever.

Inflation fell from 211 percent in 2023 to 31.5 percent in 2025, the economy grew 4.4 percent, and Argentina posted a budget surplus for two years running for the first time since 2008 ([AFP via Buenos Aires Times](https://www.batimes.com.ar/news/argentina/milei-to-reveal-second-half-agenda-in-key-congress-speech.phtml)). His party's win in the October 2025 midterms gave him enough allies in Congress to pass a long-blocked labour reform early this year, loosening hiring and firing rules in a country where union power is legendary ([Friedrich Naumann Foundation](https://www.freiheit.org/de/argentinien-brasilien-paraguay-und-uruguay/reformkurs-unter-milei-mit-starkem-start-2026)). On 1 March he declared 2026 the "year of Argentine greatness" and laid out 90 more reforms to "remake" the country for the next 50 years, pausing to call opposition lawmakers "thieves" and "parasites" ([Berliner Zeitung](https://www.berliner-zeitung.de/article/jahr-der-argentinischen-groesse-milei-kuendigt-90-reformen-fuer-sein-land-an-10021832)). The cost is real. Growth has come from farming and finance while manufacturing and retail, the biggest employers, have shrunk, and jobs and wages have replaced inflation as Argentines' top worry. Milei is closely aligned with Trump, whose government backed him with a financial lifeline in 2025. On the pitch, Argentina reached the World Cup final in July and lost to Spain in extra time.

**How power works:** A federal presidential republic. The president is directly elected for four years with one consecutive re-election; Congress has a Senate and a Chamber of Deputies, half of which is renewed every two years. The 23 provinces have strong governors who often decide whether presidents can pass laws.

**Who holds power:** President Javier Milei (La Libertad Avanza), in office since December 2023.

### Canada

Canadians have long joked that their national identity is "not American." In 2025, when Trump called Canada the "51st state" and slapped tariffs on it, the joke became a political movement: Canadians boycotted American goods and holidays, and Mark Carney, a former central banker who had never held elected office, won an election on the promise to stand up to Washington. In 2026, the fight became a trade war.

In July, Trump announced 50 percent tariffs on about $20 billion of Canadian goods, accusing Canada of "discriminatory treatment" of US dairy, alcohol and autos. After marathon talks, Carney walked away on 22 August, saying the US had demanded at the last minute that Canada restrict its trade deals with other countries. Asked if Canada was in a trade war, he said: "You're at war when you get attacked" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/8/22/carney-canada-will-enact-retaliatory-us-tariffs-starting-september-8)). Three in four Canadians backed the decision ([AFP via EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/08/25/canada-to-announce-response-to-trumps-new-tariffs)). Trump said he would double tariffs on Canadian cars to 50 percent from January. On 8 September Canada's retaliatory tariffs of up to 50 percent took effect on about C$28 billion of US goods, from steel to furniture; lobster and fresh fish were dropped after pressure from fishermen ([News on AIR](https://newsonair.gov.in/canada-imposes-retaliatory-tariffs-of-up-to-50-on-us-goods/)). Hanging over all of it is the USMCA free-trade pact, which Trump has refused to renew as is. In happier news, the Gordie Howe bridge between Windsor and Detroit finally opened in July, a reminder of how intertwined the two economies are.

**How power works:** A federal parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy, with the British king represented by a governor-general. The prime minister leads the party with the confidence of the 343-seat House of Commons; the ten provinces control health, education and natural resources.

**Who holds power:** Prime Minister Mark Carney (Liberal), in office since March 2025.

### Mexico

For two decades, Mexico's governments have fought drug cartels, and the cartels have mostly won: more than 400,000 people have been killed since 2006, and large parts of the country are governed in practice by criminal groups. In 2026, under heavy pressure from a Trump administration that designated the cartels as terrorist organisations and threatened to send US troops, Mexico struck harder than ever and found the pressure only grew.

In February, Mexican forces killed Nemesio Oseguera, "El Mencho," the leader of the Jalisco New Generation Cartel, the country's second-largest criminal group, in an operation backed by US surveillance; it was the first time forces under President Claudia Sheinbaum had taken out a cartel boss of that stature ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/30/mexico-sheinbaum)). By January, Mexico had already handed 92 alleged cartel figures to US custody. Then, on 29 April, US prosecutors in New York indicted Rubén Rocha Moya, the sitting governor of Sinaloa, and nine other officials from Sheinbaum's own Morena party, including a senator and the mayor of Culiacán, accusing them of helping the sons of "El Chapo" Guzmán in exchange for political support and bribes ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/sinaloa-governor-indicted-usmca-cartels-and-future-us-mexico-trade)). Sheinbaum demanded evidence, called the key exhibit "a sheet of paper with names and figures," and refused to extradite him; Rocha took leave and was summoned by Mexico's own prosecutors instead ([Revista E&N](https://www.revistaeyn.com/centroamericaymundo/mexico-gobierno-no-entrega-a-rocha-moya-y-lo-convoca-a-declarar-ante-justicia-nacional-MM30788400)). Analysts noted the indictment landed in the middle of talks over the USMCA trade pact, on which Mexico's economy depends. Mexico also co-hosted the World Cup, which opened in Mexico City in June.

**How power works:** A federal presidential republic. The president is directly elected for a single six-year term; Congress has a Senate and a Chamber of Deputies, both now dominated by Morena. A 2024 judicial reform made judges, including the Supreme Court, elected by popular vote.

**Who holds power:** President Claudia Sheinbaum (Morena), in office since October 2024, Mexico's first woman president.

## Asia-Pacific

### Australia

In 1998, Pauline Hanson, a former fish-and-chip shop owner who warned that Australia was being "swamped by Asians," won nearly a quarter of the vote in Queensland and shocked the political establishment. Her One Nation party then faded for years into a fringe. In 2026, it came roaring back, and the party that suffered most was not the governing Labor Party but the conservative Liberals, Australia's natural party of government for most of its history.

After a crushing loss in 2025, the Liberals' coalition with the Nationals split and their vote fell below One Nation's. In February, Angus Taylor, a former McKinsey consultant, ousted Sussan Ley as Liberal leader ([Spectator Australia](https://www.spectator.com.au/?p=10912664)). It barely helped. By June, a Resolve poll had more voters preferring Hanson as prime minister (33 percent) than Anthony Albanese (29), with Taylor on 16; a Liberal frontbencher admitted "we have broken faith with the Australian people" ([Neos Kosmos/AAP](https://neoskosmos.com/en/2026/06/15/news/liberal-mp-admits-broken-faith-amid-hanson-poll-surge/)). One Nation's support dipped after Hanson told the National Press Club Australia should be a "monoculture," but by August a RedBridge poll had it back in first place on 31 percent ([AAP via Mudgee Guardian](https://www.mudgeeguardian.com.au/story/9322349/one-nation-edges-back-in-front-in-federal-poll/)). In July, Taylor ruled out any alliance, warning a One Nation government would bring "an eternity of pain" ([The New Daily](https://thenewdaily.com.au/news/politics/australian-politics/2026/07/10/taylor-attacks-one-nation)). Albanese's own ratings have sagged over living costs and the return of so-called "ISIS brides" from Syria. Under Australia's preferential voting, Labor still leads head-to-head, but the country's two-party system is cracking.

**How power works:** A parliamentary democracy and Commonwealth realm. The prime minister leads the party with a majority in the 150-seat House of Representatives, elected by preferential voting; the Senate is elected proportionally and often decides legislation. Voting is compulsory.

**Who holds power:** Prime Minister Anthony Albanese (Labor), in office since 2022; next election due by 2028.

### China

Xi Jinping has spent 14 years making himself the most powerful Chinese leader since Mao, purging more than 200,000 officials in an anti-corruption drive that critics say doubles as a loyalty test. In 2026 the purge reached the very top of the military he commands.

On 24 January, the Defence Ministry announced that Zhang Youxia, 75, the senior vice chairman of the Central Military Commission, China's top general and a princeling whose father fought alongside Xi's, was under investigation for "serious violations of discipline and law" ([NPR via WJCT](https://news.wjct.org/national-news/2026-01-24/chinas-top-general-under-investigation-in-latest-military-purge)). The other vice chairman, He Weidong, had already been expelled in October 2025. That means both of Xi's top uniformed deputies have now fallen, along with dozens of generals, including those running the nuclear rocket force. Analysts disagree on whether this reflects real corruption, disloyalty or Xi's worry about the army's readiness for a possible Taiwan conflict, but it has left the People's Liberation Army's command in turmoil just as Xi wants it ready. In March the National People's Congress approved the 15th Five-Year Plan for 2026-30, focused on self-reliance in chips and technology and on AI as an engine of growth, to shield China from US tariffs and export controls ([TaiwanPlus](https://www.taiwanplus.com/news/taiwan-talks/international-analysis/260213011/chinas-two-sessions-military-purge-overshadows-launch-of-new-five-year-plan)). Trump visited Beijing in May, and Xi is expected in Washington this autumn. Beijing has also kept up pressure on Japan over Prime Minister Takaichi's Taiwan remarks.

**How power works:** A one-party state. The Communist Party controls the government, army, courts and media. Xi is party general secretary, state president and chairman of the Central Military Commission; the seven-member Politburo Standing Committee is the top decision body. The National People's Congress is a rubber-stamp legislature.

**Who holds power:** Xi Jinping, in power since 2012; Premier Li Qiang.

### India

West Bengal, India's fourth most populous state with more than 100 million people, gave the world Rabindranath Tagore and Satyajit Ray, and for half a century it was ruled first by Communists and then, since 2011, by the fiery regional leader Mamata Banerjee. It was the great prize that Narendra Modi's Hindu nationalist BJP had never won. In May 2026, it won it.

In state elections held in April, the BJP took 207 of 294 seats in West Bengal, ending Banerjee's 15-year rule and giving Modi's party control of almost all of northern, western and now eastern India ([Tabla](https://www.tabla.com.sg/community/opinion/editors-beat-mandate-change-and-cost-promises)). "The 2026 West Bengal Assembly Elections will be remembered forever," Modi wrote. Analysts pointed to a consolidation of Hindu votes and a controversial revision of the voter rolls by the Election Commission ([Carnegie Endowment](https://carnegieendowment.org/podcasts/grand-tamasha/flash-episode-indias-2026-elections-explained)). In Tamil Nadu, the other big upset came from cinema: Joseph Vijay, 51, one of India's biggest film stars, led his two-year-old party TVK to 108 of 234 seats, breaking a 50-year duopoly of two Dravidian parties, while sitting Chief Minister M.K. Stalin lost his own seat ([ISAS](https://www.isas.nus.edu.sg/papers/indias-2026-state-elections-upsets-in-tamil-nadu-and-west-bengal/)). With the Left also losing Kerala, no Indian state is now governed by the Communist parties that once ran three of them. Critics warn the results show power concentrating in Delhi at the expense of the federal system that holds a country of 1.4 billion and 22 official languages together.

**How power works:** A federal parliamentary republic. The prime minister leads the majority in the 543-seat Lok Sabha; the 28 states have their own elected assemblies and chief ministers who control policing, health and much more. The president is largely ceremonial.

**Who holds power:** Prime Minister Narendra Modi (BJP), in office since 2014.

### Indonesia

Prabowo Subianto, a former general once banned from the United States over human rights abuses under the Suharto dictatorship, won Indonesia's presidency in 2024 promising to feed every schoolchild. His free school meals programme, costing tens of billions of dollars, became the centrepiece of his government. In 2026 it also became a symbol of everything his critics dislike about it.

As the Iran war sent oil prices soaring, Prabowo's government raised prices of non-subsidised petrol by about 30 percent in June to ease pressure on the budget. Students marched in Jakarta and other cities, demanding a reversal and an end to the meals scheme, which has been criticised as wasteful and linked to mass food poisonings; in Surabaya, police arrested 24 protesters after clashes ([AFP via Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/728231/international/aseanphilippines/indonesian-police-arrest-dozens-in-anti-government-protest)). Protesters also objected to the growing role of the military in civilian life. The unrest echoed the August-September 2025 protests over lavish perks for lawmakers, Prabowo's worst crisis so far, which left 10 dead and thousands arrested. For the world's fourth most populous country, the question is whether a president with a huge parliamentary coalition and an authoritarian past will bend to street pressure or harden.

**How power works:** A presidential republic. The president is directly elected for five years with a two-term limit and heads the government; the House of Representatives and a regional chamber make up the legislature. Prabowo's coalition controls most of parliament.

**Who holds power:** President Prabowo Subianto, in office since October 2024.

### Pakistan

For most of the past 20 years, Washington saw Pakistan as a problem: an unreliable ally that sheltered the Taliban and hid Osama bin Laden. In 2026, it became the country America relied on to talk to Iran.

When the US and Israel attacked Iran on 28 February, Pakistan, which borders Iran and has ties to both sides, moved quickly. It brokered the 8 April ceasefire, and on 11 and 12 April hosted the first face-to-face US-Iranian talks in decades in Islamabad, with Vice President JD Vance leading the American side ([Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.tv/videos/how-pakistan-became-an-iran-war-mediator-and-what-it-means-1)). The man behind it was Field Marshal Asim Munir, the army chief, who built a close relationship with Trump after the brief India-Pakistan war in May 2025 and flew repeatedly to Tehran. Iran's ambassador said Tehran would "do talks in Pakistan and nowhere else, because we trust Pakistan" ([Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/may/22/pakistan-mediating-us-iran-war-talks/)). The White House called Pakistan the "only mediator." Yet on its other border, Pakistan was fighting its own war: from February it carried out airstrikes in Afghanistan and declared "open war" on the Taliban government over militants attacking Pakistani soldiers. At home, power has shifted decisively to the army, with Munir's elevation to chief of defence forces, while former prime minister Imran Khan, the country's most popular politician, remains in prison.

**How power works:** Formally a federal parliamentary republic, with a prime minister accountable to the National Assembly. In practice the army has ruled directly for decades at a time and remains the dominant power, shaping foreign and security policy and deciding which civilian governments survive.

**Who holds power:** Field Marshal Asim Munir, Chief of Defence Forces; Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif.

### South Korea

South Korea was a military dictatorship until 1987, and its democracy was built by people who were jailed and beaten for it. That history is why Yoon Suk Yeol's attempt to impose martial law on the night of 3 December 2024, sending troops to parliament, was met with crowds blocking soldiers and lawmakers climbing over fences to vote it down within hours. In 2026, the courts finished what the crowds started.

On 19 February, a Seoul court sentenced Yoon to life in prison for leading an insurrection, the first elected leader of the democratic era to receive the maximum custodial sentence; prosecutors had sought the death penalty ([TIME via AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/south-korean-ex-president-sentenced-073607822.html)). His former prime minister got 23 years. In June another court gave Yoon 30 more years for conspiring to send military drones over Pyongyang in October 2024 to manufacture a pretext for martial law, convicting him of aiding the enemy ([Reuters via Daily Mirror](https://www.dailymirror.lk/print/breaking-news/Ex-South-Korean-President-Yoon-Suk-Yeol-sentenced-to-30-years-in-jail-over-Pyongyang-drone-plot/108-342748)). He joins a long line of former presidents who have gone to prison, but even so has become a figurehead for South Korea's populist right. President Lee Jae-myung, whom Yoon had planned to arrest that night, has governed since June 2025, and his Democratic Party won most of the races in June's local elections.

**How power works:** A presidential republic. The president is directly elected for a single five-year term and heads the government; the 300-seat National Assembly can impeach the president, as it did Yoon, subject to the Constitutional Court.

**Who holds power:** President Lee Jae-myung (Democratic Party), in office since June 2025.

## Europe

### France

France's Fifth Republic was designed in 1958 by Charles de Gaulle to end the chaos of the Fourth, which went through more than 20 governments in 12 years. For six decades it worked. Then in 2024 Emmanuel Macron called a snap election that left parliament split into three hostile blocs, and the old instability returned: prime ministers fell one after another, including Sébastien Lecornu's first government in October 2025, which lasted 14 hours before Macron reappointed him four days later ([AFP via eNCA](https://www.enca.com/news/macron-reappoints-sebastien-lecornu-frances-pm)).

In 2026 Lecornu, 39, did what his predecessors could not: he survived. He got a 2026 budget passed in February after beating off a string of no-confidence motions, though at the price of giving up any serious attempt to cut France's deficit ([The Local](https://www.thelocal.fr/tag/politics)). In the March municipal elections the National Rally failed to take any major city. Now the country's attention has moved to the presidential election in spring 2027, when Macron must leave after two terms and the far right sees its best ever chance. Marine Le Pen, convicted of embezzling EU funds in 2025, was expected to be barred, with her 30-year-old protégé Jordan Bardella running instead; by August, Euronews reported Bardella had stepped back behind Le Pen ([Euronews](https://fr.euronews.com/2026/08/29/bardella-faure-lecornu-hollande-philippe-un-samedi-de-rentree-politique-en-france)). The centre is crowded with would-be successors: Gabriel Attal, endorsed by Macron's party in May, Édouard Philippe, Bruno Retailleau and even former president François Hollande. Lecornu insists he is not running: "I don't know what language I need to say it in!"

**How power works:** A semi-presidential republic. The directly elected president, limited to two five-year terms, controls foreign and defence policy and appoints the prime minister, who needs the tolerance of the 577-seat National Assembly. With no majority in the Assembly, every government can fall to a censure vote.

**Who holds power:** President Emmanuel Macron, until May 2027; Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu.

### Germany

After 1945, West Germany built its politics around one rule above all: never again let extremists near power. Every mainstream party has kept a "firewall" against the far-right Alternative for Germany, which the domestic intelligence service has classified as extremist. In September 2026, in one eastern state, the voters came close to knocking that wall down.

On 6 September, the AfD won 43.8 percent in Saxony-Anhalt, nearly 23 points more than in 2021, taking 39 of 83 seats, just three short of a majority, after 24 years of CDU rule there ([OSW](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/analyses/2026-09-07/germany-afd-scores-landslide-victory-saxony-anhalt)). Its 35-year-old candidate Ulrich Siegmund, a TikTok natural, promised to bring back "our good, old, safe Germany" ([Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/09/07/analysis-saxony-anhalt-election-delivers-a-blow-to-merz-and-a-warning-to-europe)). The result reflects the unhealed divide between east and west 36 years after reunification, and a rebuke of Chancellor Friedrich Merz, whose approval has sunk to record lows as his coalition struggles to revive Europe's largest economy, which barely grew for three years. Merz said the defeat shook his party "to its very foundations" but again ruled out working with the AfD; its co-leader Alice Weidel predicted he would be gone by Christmas ([Ground News](https://ground.news/article/cacdc6a6-4034-4374-9628-247bd937b331)). Whether anyone can now form a government in Magdeburg without the AfD is uncertain; the small left-populist BSW has floated a non-partisan premier relying on shifting majorities. Trump posted the result on Truth Social without comment.

**How power works:** A federal parliamentary republic. The chancellor is elected by the Bundestag and leads the government; the Bundesrat represents the 16 states, which run schools, police and much else. The president is ceremonial.

**Who holds power:** Chancellor Friedrich Merz (CDU), leading a coalition with the SPD since May 2025.

### Holy See

In May 2025, the cardinals chose the first American pope: Robert Prevost of Chicago, an Augustinian missionary who spent much of his career in Peru, who took the name Leo XIV. The name was a deliberate signal. The last Leo, Leo XIII, wrote the 1891 encyclical on the rights of workers in the industrial revolution. In 2026, Leo XIV made clear that he sees artificial intelligence as his era's equivalent.

He signed his first encyclical, ("Magnificent Humanity"), on 15 May, the 135th anniversary of , and the Vatican published it on 25 May ([Catholic World Report](https://www.catholicworldreport.com/author/hannah-brockhaus/page/2/)). It warns of a "culture of power" fuelled by the digital revolution and AI and argues that technology must serve human dignity; the Vatican invited AI researchers, including Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah, to its presentation ([Today, Explained via Podcast Rex](https://podcastrex.com/shows/today-explained/pope-leo-excommunicates)). He has since chosen AI and war as the theme of the 2027 World Day of Peace. The year's other test came from the traditionalist Society of St Pius X, which rejects the reforms of the Second Vatican Council. On 1 July, despite the pope's personal plea to "please turn back," it consecrated four bishops in the same Swiss meadow at Écône where its founder did so in defiance of Rome in 1988. The next day the Vatican declared the group in schism and excommunicated the bishops involved, reversing the outreach of Benedict XVI and Francis ([EWTN via Herald Malaysia](https://www.heraldmalaysia.com/news/vatican-confirms-excommunication-of-sspx-bishops-declares-schism/90262/2)).

**How power works:** An absolute elective monarchy. The pope, elected for life by cardinals under 80, holds full legislative, executive and judicial power over the Vatican City State and the worldwide Catholic Church of 1.4 billion members, governing through the Roman Curia.

**Who holds power:** Pope Leo XIV, since May 2025; Secretary of State Cardinal Pietro Parolin.

### Italy

Italy has had nearly 70 governments since 1946, and its prime ministers usually last about a year. Giorgia Meloni, the first woman to hold the job and leader of a party with roots in post-fascist politics, broke that pattern and became one of Europe's most stable leaders. In March 2026 she suffered her first real defeat, and it came from the voters directly.

On 22 and 23 March, Italians voted on a constitutional reform of the judiciary, the government's flagship pledge, which would have separated the careers of judges and prosecutors and reshaped the body that disciplines them. Meloni's camp said it would modernise a justice system where a civil case takes seven years on average; the magistrates and the opposition said it could put prosecutors under political control. The "No" side won with 53.7 percent, on a record referendum turnout of nearly 59 percent ([ANSA](https://www.ansa.it/english/news/2026/03/23/meloni-govt-justice-reform-defeated-in-referendum_f6c8a60d-58a5-400a-a129-2f323b3f6c98.html)). It carried almost every region except the northern strongholds of Veneto, Lombardy and Friuli. Meloni accepted the result, called it a "missed opportunity to modernise Italy," and said her government would go on; former prime minister Matteo Renzi, who resigned after losing his own referendum in 2016, called her a lame duck. The centre-left, led by Elly Schlein, was energised ahead of the general election due in 2027.

**How power works:** A parliamentary republic. The prime minister needs the confidence of both chambers, the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate; the president, elected by parliament for seven years, appoints the prime minister and can dissolve parliament. Constitutional changes not approved by two-thirds of parliament can be put to a referendum.

**Who holds power:** Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni (Brothers of Italy), in office since 2022.

### Poland

In 2023, Polish voters ousted the nationalist Law and Justice party, which the EU accused of dismantling judicial independence, and brought in Donald Tusk, a former president of the European Council, to restore the rule of law. Then in 2025 they elected a president from the other camp, Karol Nawrocki, a historian and former amateur boxer who once admitted to taking part in football hooligan brawls. In 2026, the two have fought each other to a standstill.

The Polish president does not run the government but can veto laws, and Tusk's coalition lacks the three-fifths majority to override him. Nawrocki has used that power aggressively, vetoing more legislation in his first months than his Law and Justice-aligned predecessor did in a decade ([The World This Wiki/Economist digest](https://m.douweosinga.com/projects/the_world_this_wiki/people/karol_nawrocki)). He has blocked measures from EU-backed defence loans to tech and crypto regulation, kept the courts reform the government promised stuck, and called for a new constitution by 2030 ([Polskie Radio](https://www.polskieradio.pl/395/7784/artykul/3628705,2025-in-review-a-tense-year-in-polish-politics)). He opposes Ukraine joining NATO and is demanding huge war reparations from Germany. Tusk's coalition, meanwhile, has fractured, and polls suggest Law and Justice could return in the 2027 parliamentary election. Poland matters well beyond its borders: it is NATO's front line with Russia and Belarus and the main hub for supplying Ukraine.

**How power works:** A parliamentary republic with a strong directly elected president. The prime minister leads the government with a majority in the 460-seat Sejm; the president commands the armed forces, shapes foreign policy and holds a veto that takes three-fifths of the Sejm to overturn.

**Who holds power:** Prime Minister Donald Tusk (Civic Coalition); President Karol Nawrocki, since August 2025.

### Russia

Russia has not had a competitive national election since the 1990s, but the Kremlin still holds them, carefully, because they are meant to show unity. In 2026, the first parliamentary election since Vladimir Putin's full-scale invasion of Ukraine was designed to show a country united behind the war.

From 18 to 20 September, United Russia, Putin's party, won 349 of the 450 seats in the State Duma, more than three-quarters and the most any party has ever held ([AP via National Herald](https://www.thenationalherald.com/?p=589512)). It took about 58 percent of the vote, up from under 50 percent in 2021. The only party opposed to the war, Yabloko, was barred from taking part by the Supreme Court, and no international observers from the OSCE were invited ([Northeast Times](https://northeasttimes.com/?p=163768)). The new Duma includes 46 veterans of the war in Ukraine, a sign of how the Kremlin is building a new elite out of those who fought. Putin called the result a show of public support. The vote took place under constant Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian cities and refineries, as the war passed four and a half years and Russia's economy strained under a growing budget deficit. The EU's foreign policy chief called it another setback for Russian democracy.

**How power works:** Formally a federal presidential republic; in practice an authoritarian system centred on Putin. The president is head of state, commands the military and appoints the prime minister and government. The Duma, elected by a mixed system, approves laws the Kremlin sends it. Opposition parties and independent media have been banned or driven abroad.

**Who holds power:** President Vladimir Putin, in power as president or prime minister since 1999; Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin.

### United Kingdom

In 2016, Britain voted to leave the European Union, and it has been looking for a stable leader ever since. Keir Starmer won a landslide in July 2024 promising to end the chaos. Two years later he was gone, and Britain has now had seven prime ministers in a decade, more than at any time since the early 20th century.

The collapse was fast. In May's local elections Labour lost more than 1,000 council seats as Nigel Farage's Reform UK surged, and some polls began predicting Farage as the next prime minister ([NPR via Michigan Public](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2026-06-22/keir-starmer-has-resigned-paving-way-for-a-7th-u-k-prime-minister-in-10-years)). Labour MPs turned on Starmer but had no successor in Parliament. So they made one: a Labour MP stood down, letting Andy Burnham, the popular mayor of Greater Manchester, win a by-election in Makerfield on 18 June. Starmer resigned as party leader on 22 June. Burnham was the only candidate, nominated by 379 of Labour's 403 MPs, and became prime minister on 20 July, promising "to bring back hope" and tackle falling living standards; he calls himself a socialist ([ABC News](https://abc7news.com/story/andy-burnham-becomes-uks-7th-prime-minister-10-years-meeting-king-charles-iii/19543203/)). He ran for the Labour leadership twice before and lost; this time he got it without a contest. One political analyst called him Labour's "last throw of the dice" before an election due by 2029.

**How power works:** A parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy with no written constitution. The prime minister leads the party with a majority in the 650-seat House of Commons and can change without a general election, as in July. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have devolved governments.

**Who holds power:** Prime Minister Andy Burnham (Labour), since 20 July 2026; King Charles III.

## Middle East

### Palestine

Palestinians last voted for a parliament in 2006. Hamas won, Fatah refused to accept it, and after brief civil war Hamas took Gaza in 2007, leaving Mahmoud Abbas's Palestinian Authority in charge of parts of the West Bank. Abbas was elected in 2005 to a five-year term. He is now 90 and still president. A whole generation of Palestinians has never voted. In 2026, they came close, then didn't.

Under pressure from Western and Arab governments to reform the Palestinian Authority so it could play a role in post-war Gaza, Abbas decreed in July that legislative elections would be held on 28 November, the first in nearly 20 years, with a presidential vote in early 2027 ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/palestinian-elections-background-and-implications.aspx)). A new law required every candidate to recognise the PLO as the sole representative of the Palestinian people, effectively excluding Hamas unless it changed its stance. Separately, he scheduled the first direct elections for the Palestinian National Council, the PLO's parliament, whose members have always been appointed. Then, on 9 October, Abbas postponed the legislative vote to 11 September 2027, to be held together with the presidential election, saying conditions were not right and noting that Israel had not let election materials or observers into Gaza ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/10/abbas-postpones-palestinian-legislative-elections-to-september-2027)). Gaza itself, a year after the US-brokered ceasefire, remains in limbo: Hamas still governs it, while the technocrats meant to run it under Trump's Board of Peace wait in Cairo for permission to enter ([AP via ClickOrlando](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/world/2026/10/10/mahmoud-abbas-postpones-palestinian-legislative-elections-another-year/)).

**How power works:** The Palestinian Authority, created by the 1990s Oslo Accords, governs parts of the West Bank under Israeli occupation; its parliament has not functioned since 2007, and Abbas rules by decree. Gaza is run by Hamas. The PLO represents Palestinians internationally. Palestine is a UN observer state recognised by most of the world.

**Who holds power:** President Mahmoud Abbas (Fatah), since 2005; in Gaza, Hamas.

### Saudi Arabia

Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's plan for Saudi Arabia, Vision 2030, depends on a bet: that the kingdom can turn itself from an oil state into a hub for tourism, entertainment and business, building futuristic cities in the desert. That bet needs peace. In 2022 MBS secured a truce with the Houthis in Yemen after seven years of war, and in 2023 restored relations with Iran. In 2026, the war he worked to avoid reached his cities anyway.

When the US and Israel struck Iran on 28 February, Iran fired missiles and drones at Riyadh and the oil-rich Eastern Province, targeting refineries and US forces based in the kingdom; the Saudi Formula One Grand Prix was cancelled ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_in_the_2026_Iran_war)). The bigger shock came from the south. In July, the four-year truce with the Houthis collapsed: the group announced a blockade of Saudi shipping in the Red Sea and hit the Aramco refinery in Jizan, and Saudi jets struck back in Yemen ([AFP via Aaj](https://english.aaj.tv/news/330465069)). On 8 September the Houthis launched their heaviest attack in years, striking an airbase at Khamis Mushait and oil sites in Abha, Najran and Jizan, wounding 73 people and setting installations ablaze ([Reuters via Al-Monitor](https://al-monitor.com/originals/2026/09/iran-backed-houthis-attack-four-saudi-cities-expansion-middle-east-war-73-hurt)). With the Strait of Hormuz partly blocked, Saudi Arabia's pipeline to Red Sea ports is one of the few routes for Gulf oil, which is exactly what the Houthis are now threatening.

**How power works:** An absolute monarchy with no elected legislature. The king holds supreme power; the crown prince serves as prime minister and runs the government day to day. The appointed Consultative Assembly advises only. Islamic law is the basis of the legal system.

**Who holds power:** King Salman, 90; Crown Prince and Prime Minister Mohammed bin Salman.

### Turkey

In 2019, a little-known district mayor named Ekrem İmamoğlu won Istanbul, Turkey's largest city and economic heart, ending 25 years of rule there by Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's party and its predecessors. Erdoğan himself began his rise as Istanbul's mayor, and once said whoever wins Istanbul wins Turkey. In March 2025, on the day İmamoğlu was nominated as the opposition's presidential candidate, he was arrested. In 2026, the government moved against his party too.

Since March, İmamoğlu has been on trial in a prison courtroom along with 414 co-defendants, accused of running a vast criminal network; he denies it. In May, a court annulled the 2023 congress of his Republican People's Party (CHP), Turkey's oldest party, founded by Atatürk, removing its leader Özgür Özel and reinstating his predecessor ([Turkish Minute](https://turkishminute.com/2026/07/08/jailed-istanbul-mayor-removed-from-courtroom-again-in-corruption-trial/)). Then in July, as Erdoğan hosted NATO's 36 leaders in Ankara, İmamoğlu was ejected from court after asking the judge: "How can you explain to world leaders at the NATO summit, in Turkey, in Ankara, the silencing of Ekrem İmamoğlu?" The European Parliament's rapporteur noted it was "statistically impossible" that three of his hearings fell the same day by chance ([AP via ABC News](https://abcnews.com/International/wireStory/turkeys-erdogan-hosts-nato-summit-political-rival-imamoglu-134586423)). The summit itself showcased Turkey's value to the West as NATO's second-largest army and a key player in Syria, Ukraine and the Black Sea, which critics say is why Western governments have said little.

**How power works:** A presidential republic since a 2017 referendum gave the president sweeping executive powers and abolished the post of prime minister. The president is directly elected for five years; the 600-seat Grand National Assembly makes laws but has limited oversight. Courts are widely seen as under government influence.

**Who holds power:** President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan (AKP), in power as prime minister or president since 2003; next election due 2028.
